# Design Leader Interview — Duble Slash

**Duration:** 30–40 min  
**Goal:** understand their real AI workflow + surface pain → soft pitch → qualify for 8-week pilot  
**Tone:** curious, peer-to-peer. You're a designer talking to a designer.

---

## Before the call

Pull their studio from the outreach list. Know:
- Studio size (rough headcount)
- Type of work they do (branding, product, UX, mixed)
- Any public work or named clients you can reference

Don't pre-pitch. Let the pain surface naturally before you name anything.

---

## Warm-up — 5 min

> "Before we get into it — can you tell me a bit about the studio? How big is the team, what kind of work do you mostly take on?"

Listen for: studio size, whether it's principal-led, how they bill (hourly/project/retainer), client profile.

> "And what does your role look like day-to-day — are you mostly in the work, or more managing the team?"

Listen for: IC vs. director weight. If they're still in the work, they feel the tool pain directly.

---

## Their AI reality — 10 min

> "I want to understand how AI has actually landed in your practice — not the hype version, the real version. Are you and the team using it regularly?"

Let them describe. Don't steer.

> "Which tools are you reaching for most? Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, Figma AI — anything else?"

> "Walk me through the last time AI was actually useful on a real project. What did that look like?"

Listen for: where in the process (research, concepting, copy, handoff?), whether it was solo or team, which tool.

> "And when does it go wrong? What does AI-assisted design work look like when it breaks down?"

This is the key question. Let them talk. Don't fill silence.

---

## Pain probes — 10 min

Use these only if they haven't already surfaced the pain on their own. Don't hit all four — read the conversation and pick the ones that resonate.

**Context loss / session continuity**
> "When you start a new chat on a project you've been working on — how much do you have to re-explain? What does that cost you?"

**Phase drift (AI doing too much, too fast)**
> "Have you ever asked Claude or ChatGPT to help with research or a brief, and it comes back with wireframes or a full spec you didn't ask for? How do you handle that?"

**Intensity mismatch (AI over-producing)**
> "Is there a mismatch between what you need and what the AI outputs in terms of scope? Like — you needed a quick answer and it wrote a thesis?"

**Team / handoff opacity**
> "When your team uses AI, do you have any visibility into what's happening inside those sessions? Or is it basically a black box until someone shows you the output?"

**Trust in AI output**
> "When the AI produces something — copy, a spec, a recommendation — how do you know what to trust? Do you have a way to tell what it actually decided vs. what it guessed?"

---

## The pivot — 2 min

Once you've heard real pain (you'll know it when they give a specific example or sigh):

> "Can I share something we've been building? It's directly related to what you just described."

Give the one-paragraph pitch (adapt to what they said):

> "We're building Duble Slash. It's a method and a lightweight tool that gives AI tools the context and structure to work the way designers actually work — phases, handoffs, the right level of detail for the right kind of problem. The core idea is that the AI you already use is capable enough — it just has no idea where you are in the process or how big the problem is. We give it that. The result is sessions that pick up where they left off, outputs that are sized right, and handoffs that actually transfer intent — to a teammate, to a developer, or to yourself tomorrow morning."

Then stop. Let them react.

> "Does that land? What's the part that resonates most — or doesn't?"

---

## Qualify for the pilot — 8 min

If the conversation is warm:

> "We're running an 8-week pilot with a small group of studios — completely free, hands-on. We work closely with each studio, you get early access to everything, and in exchange we ask for weekly feedback and the ability to reference you publicly once it's shipped. Does that sound like something you'd want to be part of?"

**If yes or "tell me more":**

> "A few practical questions — is AI tool spend a decision you make, or does it go through someone else?"  
> "Do you have one or two designers who are already deeper into AI workflows who'd be the natural testers?"  
> "What would make 8 weeks feel worth it for you — what would have to be true for this to have been a good use of your time?"

**If hesitant:**

> "What's the hesitation — is it timing, fit, something about the product?"

---

## Close — 2 min

**If qualified:** pin a follow-up before ending the call.

> "Let me send you a short doc that explains the pilot in detail. If it looks right, we can do a quick follow-up call with whoever else needs to be in the loop and confirm from there."

**If not the right moment:** leave the door open.

> "Totally fair. Can I keep you on the list and follow up in a month or so when we have more to show?"

---

## After the call — capture signal

Write a short note in `company/signal/` immediately after. Include:
- Studio name, date, who you talked to
- Their AI maturity (low / medium / high)
- The pain they named most specifically
- Pilot interest: yes / maybe / no
- One direct quote worth keeping

---

## What you're listening for (cheat sheet)

| Signal | What it means |
|---|---|
| Uses AI daily, across the team | High AI maturity — good cohort fit |
| Mentions losing context between sessions | Context amnesia pain — Fish handoff is the answer |
| AI drifted into work they didn't ask for | Phase drift pain — phase agents are the answer |
| Team AI work is invisible to them | Leadership pain — Digest / visibility layer |
| Bills hourly, productivity ROI is fast | Strong economic fit for the pilot |
| Principal controls tool decisions | Right buyer — no procurement maze |
| Gives you a specific example of breakdown | Real pain, not theoretical — high signal |
