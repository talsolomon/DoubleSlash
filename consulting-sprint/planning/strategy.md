## 2. The Target

**Exact profile:**
Series A B2B SaaS. 10–50 employees. Product-led or self-serve motion. Raised in the last 12 months.

**The symptoms that mean they need this NOW:**
- Free trial to paid conversion is below 15% (or they're publicly worried about it)
- Onboarding drop-off — they've tweeted, posted, or hired around it
- G2/Trustpilot/Capterra reviews that use words like "confusing," "hard to set up," "steep learning curve"
- Recent new hire in growth or marketing with a mandate to fix top-of-funnel
- Just launched a new pricing page or redesigned signup — and it's clearly broken
- They're hiring a product designer (meaning: they know they have a gap and budget is approved)

**How to find 20 of them in under 1 hour:**

**Crunchbase (25 min):**
- Filters: Company type = For Profit, Funding type = Series A, Category = SaaS + B2B, Founded last 3 years, Employee count 11–50
- Sort by most recently funded
- Export top 40, scan for product-led growth signals in their description

**Product Hunt (20 min):**
- Browse SaaS launches from last 6 months, 100–800 upvotes
- These are real products with real teams, no moat yet, founders still selling
- Click through to the product — if you can spot a UX problem in 60 seconds, that's your lead

**LinkedIn (15 min):**
- Search "we're hiring product designer" in posts from last 30 days
- Also: search job boards for "Series A SaaS" + open "Product Designer" role — budget is already approved

## 4. Sales Call Script (20 minutes)

**Opening (2 min):**
"Thanks for making time. I looked at [product] before this call — I want to ask you a few questions first so I can tell you honestly if what I do is actually the right fit. Cool?"

[This disarms and builds trust immediately. You're not selling, you're diagnosing.]

---

**4 Diagnostic Questions (8 min):**

1. "What's the specific problem you're trying to solve — not the product problem, the business problem it's creating?"
   → Surfaces urgency and helps you frame the offer in their language

2. "What have you already tried?"
   → Surfaces internal capability, previous spend, and why it hasn't worked

3. "What happens if this doesn't get fixed in the next 60 days?"
   → Creates urgency without you manufacturing it — they say it, not you

4. "Is this a decision you can make, or do we need to loop someone else in?"
   → Qualifies authority before you invest in the pitch

---

**Presenting the Offer (5 min):**

"Here's what I do. It's a 2-week sprint. Week 1 is the audit — I go through your product like a surgeon, map every drop-off point, and give you a prioritized list of what's broken and why. Week 2 is redesign — I rebuild the 3–5 screens that matter most, in Figma, production-ready, annotated for your engineers.

You walk away with a clear diagnosis AND designs you can ship. Not a strategy deck. Actual screens.

It's $12,000 flat. 50% now to hold the slot, 50% on delivery. No retainer, no scope creep."

---

**Top 3 Objections:**

**"Too expensive":**
"What's one percentage point of conversion worth to you monthly? Or what does churn cost you per quarter? This pays for itself in a week of improved retention — and you keep the designs forever. Compare that to a full-time designer hire at $120k/year who takes 6 months to ship anything."

**"We'll do it internally":**
"Totally fair. Two questions: Do you have someone with the bandwidth to do a full audit AND redesign in 2 weeks? And do they have the outside perspective to see what your team has stopped noticing? I'm not replacing your team — I'm the outside eye that gets you unstuck fast so your team can execute."

**"Send us a proposal":**
"I don't do written proposals — they take a week and usually go nowhere for both of us. What I can send you is a one-pager on the sprint structure and two case studies from similar SaaS products. But if what we've talked about today makes sense, the better question is: what's actually holding it back right now? Is it budget, timing, or do you need to loop someone in?"

"Based on what you've told me, this is the right fit. I have one sprint slot open starting [date — give a real date, 1–2 weeks out]. If we move forward today, I'll send a simple one-page agreement and an invoice for 50% to hold the slot. What do you think?"

## 5. 24-Hour Action Plan

**Goal:** 2–3 discovery calls booked by end of day.

---

**7:00am — Research (60 min)**
- Open Crunchbase: filter Series A, SaaS/B2B, funded last 12 months, 10–50 employees
- Identify 15 companies. For each: open product, do 5-min UX scan, note ONE specific problem
- Build a Google Sheet: Company | Founder/PM name | Title | Email | Problem Observed | Personalized Line

**8:00am — Write (45 min)**
- Write 10 personalized first lines using your observations (the "[flow] loses people at [specific step]" format)
- Drop each into Variant 1 template
- Subject lines: specific to their product or flow, never generic

**8:45am — Send Batch 1 (30 min)**
- Send 10 emails from your personal email address (not a tool, not a sequence — human)
- BCC yourself so you have a sent log

**9:15am — LinkedIn (45 min)**
- Find the same 10 contacts on LinkedIn
- Connection request with a 1-line note: "Used [Product] this morning — spotted something worth a quick note."
- Don't pitch in the connection request

**10:00am — Product Hunt pass (30 min)**
- Browse recent SaaS launches, find 5 more companies with visible UX problems
- Repeat the observation → email process

**10:30am — Ongoing: watch for replies**
- Reply to any response within 2 hours max
- If "tell me more" → immediately propose two call times (or Calendly link)
- If "what's the price?" → give it straight, don't hedge

**12:00pm — Send Batch 2 (30 min)**
- 5 more emails using Variant 2 (pain-first framing)

**2:00pm — LinkedIn engagement pass (30 min)**
- Comment substantively on 10 posts from target founders
- This warms your profile before they see your connection request

**5:00pm — End-of-day check**
- Count replies. Count calls booked.
- If 0 replies: tighten the subject lines, send 5 more with stronger specific observations
- If 1–2 replies: you're in the game. Follow up first thing tomorrow.
- If 3+ replies: you may need to triage — prioritize companies with the most obvious budget signals

**Success metric for Day 1:** 2 calls booked. That's it.
One of those converts at $12k = done.
