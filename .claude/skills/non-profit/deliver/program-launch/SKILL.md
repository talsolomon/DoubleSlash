## Phase 1 — Soft Launch vs. Hard Launch Decision

### Decision Framework

| Factor | Soft launch | Hard launch |
|--------|------------|------------|
| Evidence base for the model | Weak / unproven in this context | Strong — replication of proven model |
| Community trust / relationships | Still building | Established |
| Staff readiness | Need to learn delivery | Experienced, trained |
| Funder requirements | Flexible | Fixed start date in grant |
| Risk tolerance | Low — need to learn first | Higher — confidence in model |
| Media / PR interest | Low | High |

**Soft launch:** Start with a small cohort (10–20% of scale), learn, adjust, then scale. Safer. Produces better evidence.

**Hard launch:** Start at full scale. Appropriate when replicating proven model with experienced staff.

### Soft Launch Design

```
Soft launch cohort: [N beneficiaries] at [1–2 sites]
Duration: [X weeks — full program cycle]
Learning objectives:
  1. [Can we recruit target population through planned channels?]
  2. [Is the dosage achievable in practice?]
  3. [What obstacles emerge that we didn't anticipate?]
  4. [What does it actually cost per beneficiary?]
  
Documentation during soft launch:
  - Weekly facilitator debrief notes
  - Attendance and dropout tracking
  - Beneficiary feedback (informal + formal)
  - Staff time tracking (actual vs. planned)
  
Go/no-go for full launch:
  ☐ Retention ≥ [X]% during soft launch
  ☐ Staff report model is deliverable as designed
  ☐ No critical safeguarding or harm incidents
  ☐ Cost per beneficiary within [X]% of budget
```

## Phase 2 — Launch Readiness Checklist

### Pre-Launch Gates

**4 weeks before launch:**
- [ ] All staff hired and onboarded
- [ ] Facilitator training complete
- [ ] Curriculum and materials finalized and printed / uploaded
- [ ] Data collection tools tested (intake form, attendance log, pre-survey)
- [ ] Safeguarding training complete for all staff and volunteers
- [ ] Partner agreements signed
- [ ] Venue confirmed and accessible
- [ ] Beneficiary recruitment open (with enough lead time)

**2 weeks before launch:**
- [ ] Cohort 1 enrollment target met or on track
- [ ] All materials at delivery sites
- [ ] Backup facilitator identified
- [ ] First funder report template prepared
- [ ] Launch communications drafted and approved

**Launch week:**
- [ ] Beneficiary confirmation messages sent
- [ ] Emergency contacts collected
- [ ] Safeguarding reporting line communicated to participants
- [ ] First session facilitation plan confirmed
- [ ] M&E officer ready to administer baseline survey

### Enrollment Funnel

```
AWARENESS: [N] people hear about program
  ↓ [X]% express interest
INTEREST: [N] attend information session
  ↓ [X]% complete application / sign up
ENROLLED: [N] confirmed participants
  ↓ [X]% show up to first session
ACTIVE: [N] active participants
  ↓ [X]% complete program
GRADUATES: [N] program completers
```

**Target conversion rates (adjust to context):**
- Awareness → Interest: 20–40%
- Interest → Enrolled: 50–70%
- Enrolled → Active (session 1): 80–90%
- Active → Graduate: 70–85%

## Phase 6 — First-Month Monitoring Plan

### Early Warning Indicators

Monitor weekly in the first month:

| Indicator | Target | Amber | Red | Action if Red |
|-----------|--------|-------|-----|--------------|
| Session 1 attendance rate | ≥ 85% | 70–84% | < 70% | Outreach to no-shows; investigate barriers |
| Week 2 retention | ≥ 80% | 65–79% | < 65% | Dropout interviews; check engagement quality |
| Facilitator confidence (self-report) | ≥ 4/5 | 3/5 | < 3/5 | Additional coaching; curriculum support |
| Data collection completion | 100% | 90–99% | < 90% | Retrain on importance; simplify tools |
| Partner referral rate (vs. plan) | 100% | 80–99% | < 80% | Activate backup recruitment channel |

### Weekly Check-In Protocol

```
Weekly launch check-in (30 minutes):
  Attendees: PD + coordinator + facilitator(s)
  Agenda:
    1. Attendance and retention this week (5 min)
    2. What went well — keep doing (5 min)
    3. What needs adjustment — decide now (10 min)
    4. Blockers — what needs escalation (5 min)
    5. Next week priorities (5 min)
    
Documentation: Brief notes captured in shared log
Action items: Named, dated, tracked to next check-in
```

### Adaptive Management Triggers

Define in advance what level of deviation triggers program adaptation:

| If this happens | Response | Decision authority |
|----------------|----------|-------------------|
| Attendance < 60% for 2+ consecutive sessions | Review recruitment + curriculum; consider schedule change | Program Director |
| Facilitator flags curriculum not working | Convene 48-hour review; adapt or substitute session | PD + curriculum lead |
| Safeguarding incident | Immediate report to DSL; follow safeguarding protocol | Designated Safeguarding Lead |
| Partner stops delivering | Activate backup plan; communicate to funders | PD + ED |
| Funder asks for unplanned information | Prepare response within 5 business days | PD + ED |

## Voice

Unapologetically brilliant. You're an expert in this domain who speaks as a trusted friend and mentor — not a consultant, not a chatbot.

Give the real answer. Say what you'd actually do. Don't hedge when you know. Don't list options when one is clearly better. When you don't know, say so directly — then give your best read anyway.

Never:
- "As an AI, I..." 
- "There are several approaches we could consider..."
- Bullet-point things that should be said in a sentence
- Add caveats that weren't asked for
