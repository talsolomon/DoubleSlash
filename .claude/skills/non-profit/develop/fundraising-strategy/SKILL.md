## Phase 2 — Major Gift Strategy

### Major Gift Pyramid

Construct a gift pyramid showing how many donors at each level produce the total target:

```
MAJOR GIFT PYRAMID

Transformational gifts (≥ $100K): [N] donors × $[Avg] = $[X]
Leadership gifts ($25K–$99K): [N] donors × $[Avg] = $[X]
Major gifts ($10K–$24K): [N] donors × $[Avg] = $[X]
Mid-level gifts ($1K–$9K): [N] donors × $[Avg] = $[X]
Small donors (< $1K): [N] donors × $[Avg] = $[X]
Total from individuals: $[X]
```

**Conversion ratios (benchmarks):**
- Cultivation contacts to first major gift ask: 5–10 meaningful touchpoints
- First ask to gift: 30–50% conversion rate (higher with warm relationship)
- Annual renewal rate for major donors: 70–85% (strong programs); 50–65% (typical)

### Major Gift Cultivation System

| Stage | Description | Time | Activities |
|-------|-------------|------|-----------|
| Identification | Prospect meets wealth and affinity criteria | Ongoing | Wealth screening, board nominations, donor upgrades |
| Qualification | Confirm interest and capacity | 1–3 months | Informational conversation, event invite |
| Cultivation | Deepen relationship before ask | 3–18 months | Site visit, program update, peer introduction, advisory role |
| Solicitation | Make the ask | One meeting | Personalized proposal; ask with a specific amount for a specific purpose |
| Stewardship | Deliver on commitment; earn renewal | Ongoing | Reports, access, recognition, renewal strategy |

### Cultivation Tracking

| Prospect | Relationship stage | Last contact | Next contact | Ask amount | Ask date | Staff owner |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|-------------|----------|---------|------------|
| [Name] | Cultivation | [Date] | [Date + action] | $[X] | [Quarter] | [Name] |

### Ask Structure

```
The ask: "Would you consider a gift of $[specific amount] to support [specific program/purpose]?"

Why specific amount: Range asks ("$10K–$50K") invite the lower number.
Why specific purpose: Restricted gifts are often easier than unrestricted.
Why ask directly: Ambiguity signals lack of confidence; directness respects the donor.

Follow-up: If answer is "I need to think about it" — 
  "Of course. Can we plan to follow up in [2 weeks]?"
  Do not leave without a next step.
```

### Grant Proposal Quality Standards

**Strong grant proposals:**
1. **Problem statement:** Quantified with data; local specificity matters
2. **Theory of change:** Clear if-then logic; not just activities
3. **Program description:** Specific enough that a funder could monitor it
4. **Evaluation plan:** Indicators with baselines and targets; data collection method
5. **Organizational capacity:** Why we can do this — track record, staff, systems
6. **Budget:** Aligned to activities; reasonable indirect rate; no budget padding

**Common rejection reasons:**
- Misalignment with funder priorities (verify before applying)
- Weak evidence for the approach
- No evaluation plan
- Vague outcomes (outputs, not outcomes)
- Budget doesn't match activities

### Grant Calendar Management

```

  Renewal grants require: Funder reports delivered on time + strong outcomes
  Renewal rate target: ≥ 70% of existing grants renewed
```

## Phase 4 — Individual Giving Strategy

### Donor Funnel

```
AWARENESS
  ↓ [% become interested]
FIRST GIFT (acquisition)
  ↓ [% give again within 12 months — first-year retention]
REPEAT GIVING (retention)
  ↓ [% upgrade over 3 years]
MAJOR GIFT (upgrade)
  ↓ [% include org in estate plans]
PLANNED GIVING (legacy)
```

**Sector benchmarks:**
- First-year donor retention: 23–45% (first gift is hardest to retain)
- Multi-year donor retention: 60–70%
- Upgrade rate (annual giving increase): 15–25%
- Lapsed donor recapture: 5–10% with targeted reactivation

### Acquisition Channels

| Channel | Cost per new donor | Avg first gift | Lifetime value | Break-even (years) |
|---------|-------------------|---------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Digital advertising | $[X] | $[Y] | $[LTV] | [Years] |
| Peer-to-peer fundraising | $[X] | $[Y] | $[LTV] | |
| Events (new attendees) | $[X] | $[Y] | $[LTV] | |
| Direct mail (acquisition) | $[X] | $[Y] | $[LTV] | |
| Organic / referral | ~$0 | $[Y] | $[LTV] | Immediate |

**Lifetime value (LTV)** = Average annual gift × Retention rate ÷ (1 − Retention rate)
Example: $150/yr gift × 65% retention rate ÷ 35% = **$278 LTV**

### Retention and Upgrade Strategy

| Donor segment | Retention tactic | Upgrade tactic | Frequency |
|--------------|-----------------|---------------|---------|
| < $500 (small donor) | Thank-you within 48 hrs; impact report | Recurring giving ask | 2× touch/year |
| $500–$5K (mid-level) | Personal thank-you call; site visit invite | Mid-level society or naming | 4× touch/year |
| $5K–$25K (leadership) | Personal note from ED; annual check-in | Major gift cultivation | 6+ touch/year |
| Recurring donors | Impact report; annual upgrade ask | Annual amount increase | Monthly + annual |

## Phase 5 — Case for Support

### Case for Support Framework

The case for support answers three questions every donor asks:
1. **Why does this problem matter?** (Urgency + scale)
2. **Why is your approach the right one?** (Evidence + differentiation)
3. **Why give now, to you?** (Opportunity + organizational credibility)

### Case for Support Template

```markdown
**The Problem** (1 paragraph)
[Specific problem + scale + urgency + why it persists]
"[N] people in [geography] face [problem]. Without intervention, [consequence]. 
Despite existing services, [gap — why it persists]."

**Our Approach** (1–2 paragraphs)
[What we do + why it works + evidence]
"[Organization] works with [population] to [what we do]. Our approach is grounded in 
[evidence/theory]. Participants who complete the program [specific outcome with data]."

**Our Track Record** (1 paragraph)
[Results + credibility + growth]
"Since [year], we have served [N] people across [geography]. In [most recent year], 
[% or # achieving outcome]. We have grown from [X] to [Y] because [reason]."

**The Opportunity** (1 paragraph)
[Why give now + what the gift will do]
"With your support, we will [specific action] which will [specific outcome] for [N] people. 
A gift of $[X] will [what it funds]. We have never been better positioned to [goal]."

**Call to Action**
"We invite you to join us by [specific ask]."
```

## Phase 6 — Board Fundraising Engagement

### Board Fundraising Roles

Not all board members fundraise the same way. Assign roles by strength:

| Role | Who | What it means | Ask |
|------|-----|--------------|-----|
| Prospect identifier | All board members | Submit 5–10 names annually for staff follow-up | Annual list submission |
| Connector | Networked board members | Make introductions to prospects; host cultivation events | 2–3 introductions/year |
| Asker | Comfortable with direct asks | Join staff on major gift visits | [X] asks/year |
| Spokesperson | Mission-passionate communicators | Tell the story at events, media | [X] appearances |
| Personal gift | All board members | 100% board participation (funders check this) | Give meaningfully |

### Board Give/Get Policy

```
Policy: Every board member gives AND gets [X] per year.
  Give: Personal gift at a level meaningful to them (not a fixed amount — 
        $100 from someone living paycheck-to-paycheck equals $10,000 from someone wealthy)
  Get: Secure [X amount] from external sources through introductions, asks, events

100% board participation is non-negotiable — funders use this as a proxy for board confidence in leadership.
```

### Board Fundraising Calendar

| Month | Board fundraising activity |
|-------|--------------------------|
| Jan | Annual board gift pledge / kickoff |
| Mar | Prospect list submission due |
| May | Gala / major event — board sells tables |
| Sep | Major gift asks — board joins staff on visits |
| Nov | Year-end appeal — board sends personal notes to top donors |
| Dec | Board gives personally before year-end |
