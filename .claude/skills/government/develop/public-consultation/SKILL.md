## Phase 3 — Consultation Question Design

### Question Design Principles

1. **Start with impact questions** — "How will this affect you?" before "What do you think we should do?"
2. **Separate fact from opinion** — "What is your current cost of compliance?" (fact) vs. "Is this cost proportionate?" (opinion)
3. **Use open questions for design issues, closed for scoping** — open generates insight, closed generates countable responses
4. **Tell them what you'll do with the answer** — "Your response to this question will directly inform [specific design element]"
5. **Maximum 10–12 questions** — longer surveys get abandoned

### Standard Consultation Question Set (adapt per policy)

```
Section 1 — About you (2–3 questions)
Q1. Which of the following best describes your organization/situation? [Multiple choice]
Q2. How does [policy area] affect you / your organization? [Open]

Section 2 — Impact assessment (3–4 questions)
Q3. What aspects of the proposed [regulation/policy/program] would have the greatest positive impact? [Open]
Q4. What aspects are you most concerned about, and why? [Open]
Q5. We estimate compliance cost at [$X]. Is this consistent with your experience? [Scale + open]

Section 3 — Design input (2–3 questions)
Q6. Are there alternative approaches that would achieve the same objective with lower burden? [Open]
Q7. Are there practical implementation issues the design does not address? [Open]

Section 4 — Priority groups (optional — targeted to specific segments)
Q8. [Specific to regulated entities / service users / etc.]

Section 5 — Overall (1 question)
Q9. Is there anything else you would like to tell us about [policy area] that we haven't asked? [Open]
```

### Closed/Scale Question Format

```
Question: [Statement about the policy]
Response scale: Strongly agree | Agree | Neither agree nor disagree | Disagree | Strongly disagree
Follow-up: [If disagree/strongly disagree] Please explain your concern: [Open text]
```

## Phase 4 — Channel Mix Design

### Channel Selection Matrix

| Channel | Best for | Reach | Cost | Feedback depth |
|---------|----------|-------|------|----------------|
| Online submission portal | Formal stakeholders, general public | High | Low | Medium |
| Written submission (email/mail) | Professional/industry, detailed technical | Medium | Low | High |
| Public meetings / town halls | Community, geographic engagement | Medium | High | Medium |
| Targeted roundtables | Specific expert groups | Low | Medium | Very high |
| Surveys (online / phone) | General public, quick pulse | High | Medium | Low |
| In-person facilitated workshops | Complex policy, co-design | Low | High | Very high |
| Social media listening | General public sentiment | Very high | Low | Very low |

### Recommended Channel Mix (standard policy consultation)

```
Primary channel:  Online submission portal (main mechanism)
Expert input:     Roundtables with 3–5 key stakeholder groups
Community:        2–4 regional/community forums for affected groups
General public:   Optional survey for those who won't make formal submissions
Accessibility:    Phone option with assisted completion
```

## Phase 6 — Response Synthesis Methodology

### Quantitative Analysis

**For closed/scale questions:**
- Calculate % response at each scale point
- Cross-tabulate by respondent category (regulated entity vs. general public vs. advocacy)
- Weight by affected population share (not by submission volume)

**Submission counting rules:**
- Coordinated campaigns (multiple identical/near-identical submissions): count as one position with N signatories
- Identify and flag organized campaigns vs. independent responses
- Weight is not purely numeric — 1 well-evidenced technical submission may outweigh 1,000 form letters

### Qualitative Analysis

**Thematic coding approach:**
1. Read a sample of 20–30 submissions to develop an initial coding framework
2. Apply codes to all submissions
3. Report: theme, frequency, representative quote, stakeholder type raising it
4. Flag themes unique to specific stakeholder categories

### Response Analysis Template

```
Theme: [Name]
Frequency: [N submissions / % of total]
Stakeholder types raising it: [Regulated entities / community / expert]
Representative quote: "[Direct quote from submission]"
Policy design implication: [What we need to consider or change]
```

## Phase 7 — Policy Update Decision Process

### Pre-Commitment Framework (set before consultation opens)

Before opening consultation, formally decide:

| Question | Decision | Rationale |
|----------|----------|-----------|
| What aspects are open to change based on consultation? | [List specifically] | [Why these are genuinely open] |
| What aspects are fixed regardless of consultation? | [List specifically] | [Legal obligation, political commitment, etc.] |
| What is the decision rule for changes? | [Threshold: e.g., "significant evidence from affected parties"] | |
| Who decides what changes are made? | [Named decision-maker] | |

**Transparency requirement:** Publish this pre-commitment in the consultation documentation. If you won't change something, say so — it builds more trust than false openness.

### Post-Consultation Policy Update Decision

For each theme raised in consultation:

```
Theme: [X]
# of submissions raising it: [N]
Stakeholder types: [Who raised it]
Our assessment:
  — Evidence quality: [Strong/Moderate/Weak]
  — Materiality to policy goal: [High/Medium/Low]
Decision: [Change policy / Provide explanation / Reject — reason]
If change: [Specific amendment to policy design]
```
