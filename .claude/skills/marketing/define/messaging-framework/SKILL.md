### Step 1 — Positioning Statement (Geoffrey Moore Formula)

Fill in the template, then test it:

```
TEMPLATE:
For [target customer]
who [statement of need or opportunity],
[product name] is a [product category]
that [key benefit / compelling reason to buy].
Unlike [primary competitive alternative],
our product [statement of primary differentiation].

FILLED:
For ___________
who ___________,
[product] is a ___________
that ___________.
Unlike ___________,
our product ___________.

QUALITY TEST:
□ Specific enough that a competitor couldn't use it word-for-word?
□ Does it name a real need, not a feature?
□ Is the differentiation claim actually true and provable?
□ Would a target buyer recognize themselves in "target customer"?
□ Is the category label the one buyers already use?
```

### Step 2 — Value Proposition Canvas

For each of the top 3 value propositions:

```
Value Prop: [Name / label]
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Customer job addressed:  [What they're trying to accomplish]
Customer pain relieved:  [Friction, cost, risk, or frustration removed]
Customer gain created:   [New capability, result, or feeling enabled]

Our claim:   [One sentence — what we do about this]
Proof point 1: [Specific — stat, case study, feature, or demo]
Proof point 2: [Specific]
Proof point 3: [Specific]

Believability test: Can we prove this claim in 60 seconds? [Yes / No / With caveat]
```

### Step 3 — Message Hierarchy

The full message ladder, from concept to copy:

```
Campaign theme:      [The big idea — 3–5 words. What this all adds up to.]
Tagline:             [The short memorable line. Optional but powerful if you have one.]

Level 1 — Positioning (for strategic docs, investor decks):
  [Full positioning statement from Step 1]

Level 2 — Elevator pitch (30 seconds, spoken):
  "[Name] helps [who] [do what] without [pain]. Unlike [alternative], we [key diff]."

Level 3 — Homepage headline (6–10 words):
  Option A: [Outcome-led — what they get]
  Option B: [Problem-led — what they escape]
  Option C: [Category-reframe — how we see the world differently]

Level 4 — Sub-headline (1–2 sentences):
  [Expands on the headline with one concrete detail that makes it credible]

Level 5 — CTA:
  Primary:   [Specific action — "Start free" / "See it work" / "Book a demo"]
  Secondary: [Lower-commitment option — "See pricing" / "Read case study"]
```

### Step 4 — Competitive Differentiation Map

```
Differentiation analysis:
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Attribute             Us     Competitor A  Competitor B  Competitor C
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[Key attribute 1]    [★★★]   [★★]          [★★★]         [★]
[Key attribute 2]    [★★★]   [★★★]         [★]           [★★]
[Key attribute 3]    [★★]    [★]           [★]           [★★★]
[Key attribute 4]    [★★★]   [★★]          [★★]          [★]
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
★★★ = clear leader  ★★ = competitive  ★ = weak/absent

Our owned territory:    [The 1–2 attributes where we clearly lead]
Parity zone:            [Where we're competitive but not differentiated]
Avoid claiming:         [Where competitors clearly beat us — don't fight here]

Competitive message response:
  "We're just like [Competitor A]": "[Response — what makes us different]"
  "Why not just use [Competitor B]": "[Response]"
  "We already use [Competitor C]": "[Response for displacement]"
```

### Step 5 — Objection Handling Guide

```
Objection 1: "[Most common reason they don't buy]"
  Root fear behind it:  [What they're really worried about]
  Our response:         [Acknowledge + reframe + prove]
  Supporting evidence:  [Proof point — stat, case study, demo, guarantee]

Objection 2: "[Price / ROI objection]"
  Root fear behind it:  [What they're really worried about]
  Our response:         [Acknowledge + reframe + prove]
  Supporting evidence:  [Cost comparison, ROI calculation, payback period]

Objection 3: "[Switching cost / implementation concern]"
  Root fear behind it:  [What they're really worried about]
  Our response:         [Acknowledge + reframe + prove]
  Supporting evidence:  [Onboarding time, migration support, case study]

Objection 4: "[Incumbent / status quo objection — "we're fine with what we have"]"
  Root fear behind it:  [Switching risk, political cost]
  Our response:         [Cost of inaction framing]

```

### Step 6 — Audience-Specific Message Variants

For each ICP, adapt the core message to their specific trigger and language:

```

  Trigger-specific headline:  [Speaks directly to their buying trigger]
  Language to use:            [Terms, verbs, and frames this audience uses]
  Language to avoid:          [Jargon or frames that signal you don't get them]
  Proof point that lands:     [The specific type of evidence this ICP trusts most]
  CTA for this audience:      [The specific action that fits their buying stage]
  Channel-specific variant:   [How the message adapts for their primary channel]
```
